How the SLA credit calculation works

counted breaches = breached − excluded
compliance % = (total − counted breaches) / total × 100 (excluded cycles count as met)
Per-breach rule: credit = counted breaches × amount per breach
Tiered rule: credit = fee × tier % / 100
Then credit = min(credit, fee × cap % / 100), rounded half-up to the cent.

How to calculate SLA service credits, step by step

An SLA (service level agreement) credit, also called a service credit or SLA penalty, is the reduction of the fee that a provider owes its customer when agreed service levels are missed. Most contracts express it in one of two ways: a percentage of the monthly fee that grows as compliance drops (a tiered schedule), or a fixed amount for each breach. Either way, the arithmetic is simple, but the details of the clause change the result, so it is worth doing it carefully.

1. Measure SLA compliance

Start from the period the contract uses, usually a calendar month. Count the SLA cycles measured (for a help desk, often the tickets with a response or resolution target) and the cycles that breached their target. Remove the breaches the contract excludes, such as delays caused by the customer, planned maintenance or force majeure. Compliance is then (total cycles − counted breaches) / total cycles × 100. For an availability SLA, the same logic applies to minutes of uptime instead of tickets.

2. Find the applicable credit

With a tiered schedule, find the lowest threshold that compliance falls below and apply its percentage to the monthly fee of the affected service. With a per-breach rule, multiply the counted breaches by the agreed amount. Check whether the fee is the whole contract or only the affected service: many clauses apply the credit to the affected component only.

3. Apply the cap and the claim rules

Almost every SLA caps credits, for example at 20 % or 50 % of the monthly fee. Take the smaller of the computed credit and the cap. Some contracts also require the customer to claim the credit within a set number of days, or say that credits are the sole remedy; the calculator does not check those conditions.

Worked example

A provider charges 10,000 per month. In October, 400 tickets had an SLA, 28 breached and 4 of those breaches were caused by the customer and are excluded. Counted breaches: 28 − 4 = 24. Compliance: (400 − 24) / 400 = 94.00 %. The contract grants 5 % below 99 %, 10 % below 95 % and 25 % below 90 %, so the 10 % tier applies: 10,000 × 10 % = 1,000. The cap is 20 % of the fee, or 2,000, which is not reached. The credit owed is 1,000.00. Under a per-breach rule of 50 per breach, the same month would give 24 × 50 = 1,200, still under the cap.

Two points cause most disagreements: whether excluded breaches are removed from the total or counted as met (this calculator counts them as met), and how amounts are rounded. Read your clause on both before sending a figure to a customer or a provider. If you work in Jira Service Management, the SLA report gives you the met and breached counts per month to enter above. FAQ

What is an SLA credit? A contractual reduction of the fee when the provider misses agreed service levels.

Should excluded cycles reduce the total? Contracts differ. This calculator treats them as met; check your contract wording.
